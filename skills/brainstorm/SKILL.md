# Brainstorming

Turn an early idea into an agreed direction through focused conversation. Brainstorming explores; it does not write the plan or implement it.

## Start with context

Inspect enough of the owning project to understand what already exists, what constraints are real, and whether the idea overlaps prior work. Scale the inspection to the question.

## Explore

- Clarify the problem, desired outcome, users, constraints, and success criteria.
- Ask one useful question at a time.
- Separate facts, assumptions, and open decisions.
- When several approaches are genuinely viable, present the smallest meaningful set with tradeoffs and a recommendation.
- Push back when a simpler framing or solution better serves the outcome.
- Use a visual only when it materially clarifies a relationship that prose does not.

Do not force formal design ceremony onto a settled or trivial decision. Do not create speculative requirements merely to make the idea feel complete.

## Resolve uncertainty

Ask the user when a material ambiguity, conflict, or unstated preference must be resolved before choosing a direction. Brainstorming may expose those questions; it must not answer them on the user's behalf.

## Converge

Finish when there is shared understanding of:

- the problem and intended outcome;
- the selected direction and why it was chosen;
- relevant constraints and explicit non-goals;
- what success will look like;
- any remaining uncertainty that must stay visible.

Summarize that agreement concisely. When it defines project work, it becomes issues in the repo's tracker, not a separate design or plan document. Do not begin implementation during brainstorming.
